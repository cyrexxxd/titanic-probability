Probability on the Titanic

An interactive lab in probability theory, built on the passenger list of RMS Titanic (1912).

Every number on this page is computed in your browser from the actual passenger list of the Titanic, with no prepared answers. The experiment behind each probability is always the same: choose one of the 1,309 passengers uniformly at random, and ask how likely the chosen passenger is to have a given property. Classical probability, the addition and multiplication rules, independence, total probability and Bayes' theorem, and random variables are all applied to this single dataset, so formulas and data can be checked against each other at every step.

1The data

The dataset is titanic3, maintained by the Vanderbilt University Department of Biostatistics. It lists 1,309 passengers (the crew is not included) with ticket class, sex, age, family relations, fare and port of embarkation, plus whether the person survived the sinking on 15 April 1912.

Caveats

  • Age is missing for 263 passengers (about one in five). Events built on age therefore use only passengers with a known age, and “age unknown” is treated as its own group.
  • Fare is missing for 1 passenger; port of embarkation is missing for 2. Rows with a missing value are excluded from calculations that need that variable.
  • These are historical records, not a random sample of any larger population: every probability on this page is computed for this list, not an estimate for Edwardian ocean travel in general.

Independence is an assumption about the model, not about the people. Real Titanic survival was not independent across passengers: families went to the lifeboats together, so one family member surviving made others more likely to survive. That is exactly the lecture's “crooked gambler” situation: independence fails whenever a hidden common factor (here: family membership, ticket class, access to boats) links the trials. The independence calculations in §6 are therefore a deliberate modelling assumption, which is why each result is labelled as such.
